## 5.9 Alternative Approaches and Weighted Decision Matrix (draft v1, 25 Nov 2026)

**Owner.** Cemal Batuhan Baş (System Architect).
**Inputs.** Project card (R1–R9, QR check-in), 5.4 (NFRs), 5.5 (schedule), 5.8 (chosen architecture and stack), 5.10 (feasibility, KVKK), 5.11 (operating costs). External facts are listed under Sources with the date they were read.
**Method.** Three alternatives are compared on six criteria. Each weight is tied to a requirement or a finding in an earlier section. Each score is on a 1–5 scale (1 = poor, 5 = very good). **The scores are the team's judgement**, based on the reasons written in each cell. They are not measurements. A sensitivity check at the end shows how much the result depends on the weights.

### 5.9.1 The three alternatives

| | Alternative A (chosen) | Alternative B | Alternative C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Web app with REST API and relational database | Mobile app with a backend-as-a-service | Low-code platform |

**Result: Alternative A (4.40) is chosen.** B (2.55) and C (2.50) are close to each other and well below A.

### 5.9.5 Sensitivity check: does the result depend on the weights?

| Weighting | A | B | C | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proposed weights (5.9.2) | 4.40 | 2.55 | 2.50 | A |
| All six criteria equal (1/6 each) | 4.33 | 2.67 | 2.50 | A |
| "Mobile first": K4 doubled to 30%, K1 and K2 reduced to 15% each | 4.30 | 2.85 | 2.60 | A |

**Break-even for B.** B is not better than A on any criterion; it is equal on K4. Even if B were given 5 on K4 (one point more than A), the K4 weight would have to be above about 68% for B to win. (A − B on the other five criteria is 2.18 points on average with the proposed weights; 2.18 × (1 − w) = 1 × w gives w ≈ 0.685.) No requirement in 5.4 supports such a weight.

**What the check does not prove.** A leads on every criterion, so the result depends more on the **scores** than on the weights. The scores are the team's judgement. The cells above give the reason for each score so that a reader can disagree with a specific cell. The cells marked [TBD] are the ones with the least evidence.

### 5.9.6 What we give up by choosing A

- **No native app.** B would scan QR codes through the phone's camera directly and could send push notifications. A uses the browser camera and in-app notifications (F4). If the QR library tested in H23 does not work well on the team's phones, this is the first risk to look at.
- **More code to write.** C would produce simple forms faster. A needs the backend API to be written before the code freeze (H26, H33), which is on the critical path in 5.5.
- **Hosting is our job.** B and C are managed platforms. With A, the team chooses and configures the hosts (5.7 Deployment), including the sleeping free tier risk (H24).

### 5.9.7 When would the decision change?

- If the university required a **native app in the stores**, B would become a candidate again. The REST API of A could still be used by a later Flutter app, so the decision does not close that door.
- If the university already licensed **Power Apps or AppSheet for all students** and allowed the team to use it, the cost score of C would rise. The rule and testing scores would not change.
