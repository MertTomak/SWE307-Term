## 5.1 Executive Summary

**Status note (to be revisited before the final PDF).** This summary is written on 25 Nov 2026, while 5.2, 5.3, 5.6, 5.9 and 5.12 are still in progress and the backend API has not been built. The figures below come from the sections that are finished (5.4, 5.5, 5.8, 5.10, 5.11). It should be re-read once 5.12's test results and screenshots exist, so that no claim here is contradicted by the final implementation.

**The problem.** Classrooms, labs and equipment at the university are booked informally — by phone, e-mail or a shared spreadsheet — which creates double bookings, gives the Resource Admin no enforcement over no-shows, and produces no usage data for planning. There is no way to check in at the resource, no automatic release of a slot nobody used, and no record of how often a resource actually sits empty after being booked.

**The proposed solution.** CampusSlot is a web application that lets students and academic staff search, filter and reserve rooms, labs and equipment, check in with a QR code or a 6-digit fallback code, and frees a slot automatically if nobody checks in within 15 minutes of the start time. Nine business rules (R1–R9) fixed by the project card govern the system end to end: a 3-hour reservation limit (R1), role-based active-reservation limits of 2 for students and 5 for academic staff (R2), no overlapping reservations for the same resource (R3), a 7-day booking ban after 3 no-shows in 30 days (R4, R9), mandatory admin approval for equipment (R5), cancellation until the start time (R6), and the QR/no-show mechanics (R7, R8). The functional scope is fixed at 55 functional requirements (FR-01 to FR-55) across 8 features, F1–F8 (5.4).

**Technical approach.** The system is a modular monolith: a React + Vite + TypeScript client, an Express + TypeScript API with 23 REST endpoints, and PostgreSQL through Prisma, with a database-level exclusion constraint enforcing R3 even under concurrent requests (5.8). Authentication uses JWT with three roles (Campus User split into Student/Academic Staff, and Resource Admin). Security controls follow OWASP Top 10:2025, and the data-privacy design follows KVKK: data minimisation (only name, e-mail, password hash and role are stored), a documented retention and destruction rule, and an open point on cross-border hosting (Art. 9) that is resolved for the pilot by using only fictional seed data (5.8.5, 5.10.2).

**Process and schedule.** The team chose Scrum over Waterfall and Spiral (5.5.1) for a 19-calendar-day delivery window (18 Nov – 6 Dec 2026), split into two one-week sprints and a stabilisation week. Function Point Analysis sizes the system at 104 adjusted function points (≈5.5 KLOC); Basic COCOMO estimates 14.4 person-months of effort against a real plan of about 4.4–4.7 person-months for a team of 8 (seven from kickoff, one who joined later) over 19 days — a 3.0–3.3× gap that the team reads as a warning to protect the fixed scope and the 1 December code freeze, not as a reason to add people (5.5.8). Critical path analysis gives an 18-day project duration against 19 available days (1-day buffer), with an estimated 88.5% probability of finishing on time and about 75% of meeting the code freeze (5.5, 5.10.3).

**Feasibility.** Technical, legal and schedule feasibility are each assessed as feasible with conditions: a mature, team-familiar stack and HTTPS for QR access (technical); data minimisation, a proposed retention rule and an unresolved cross-border hosting question (legal, 5.10.2); and a 1-day schedule buffer that depends on keeping scope fixed (schedule, 5.10.3). Operational feasibility awaits the fact-finding survey due 25 Nov 2026 (5.10.4).

**Expected value.** The cost–benefit analysis (5.11) compares a 77,211 TL one-off development cost (the team's own time, valued as an opportunity cost) against a 61,791 TL/year operating cost and a 116,175 TL/year benefit from administrative time saved on bookings, at a 36% nominal discount rate (the TCMB policy rate). Over 3 years the base case gives a positive but thin NPV of +13,799 TL, a 7.6% discounted ROI, and a 2.4-year discounted payback. The result is decided almost entirely by one unmeasured input — how many minutes of staff time a manual booking takes today — and the NPV changes sign between 8 and 10 minutes (5.11.11). Economic feasibility is therefore stated as feasible with conditions: the conclusion holds in every tested scenario except the lowest end of that range, and the pending fact-finding survey is what turns this from a model into a finding (5.10.5).

**Main results so far.**
- A fixed, traceable scope: 55 functional requirements and 20 non-functional requirements, each linked to a business rule, a use case or a schema field (5.4).
- A sized and estimated project (104 AFP, 14.4 person-month COCOMO estimate) compared honestly against the real 19-day plan, with the resulting schedule risk made explicit rather than hidden (5.5).
- A designed and partly built system: data model, API contract, security design and a frontend skeleton with working Login and Register screens exist on `main`; the backend API, the remaining frontend screens and the system scheduler are the main work still outstanding against the 1 December code freeze.
- A positive, conditional economic case, with the single input that would flip it identified and handed to the team's fact-finding work rather than assumed away.

**Who benefits.** Resource Admins recover staff time currently spent on manual booking; students and academic staff get a self-service calendar, fair first-come access and automatic no-show release; university management gains occupancy and no-show data that does not exist today; and the university, as data controller, gets a documented KVKK position instead of an undocumented one (5.11.12).
