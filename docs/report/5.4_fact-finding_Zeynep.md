### 5.4.1 Overview of the techniques

Four techniques were used, so that each finding can be checked from more than one side (triangulation): what students *say* (survey), what the staff who run the process *say* (interviews), what actually *happens* at the rooms (observation) and what is *written down* (documents).

| Technique | Purpose | Participants / object | Instrument | When | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survey (questionnaire) | Measure how often students need resources, how hard it is to find one, how common no-shows are, and which features they want. | Students of our university; target at least 30 responses. | Google Forms, 12 questions plus a consent question (Appendix A). | 26–28 Nov 2026 | Appendix A: questions, aggregated results and charts. |
| Interview (semi-structured) | Understand the current process from the staff side and collect figures that users cannot give (requests per week, minutes per request, no-show rate). | INT-01: laboratory staff member responsible for lab and equipment bookings. INT-02: library staff member responsible for the library's existing room booking system. | Interview guides with 10 questions each plus 5 numerical questions (Appendix B). | 27–28 Nov 2026 | Appendix B: interview records and summaries confirmed by the interviewees. |
| Observation (non-participant) | See whether reserved or scheduled rooms are really used, and whether students search for free places. | 4–6 classrooms or labs visible from one corridor, during one busy hour. | Observation form with five snapshots at 15-minute intervals and an event log (Appendix C). | 27 or 28 Nov 2026 | Appendix C: completed observation form. |
| Document review | Learn the rules, data fields and steps that the current process already has in writing. | Booking forms, loan registers, door schedules, lab rules, university directives, the library system's screens, the university's KVKK privacy notice. | Document list (DOC-01 to DOC-09) and review checklist (Appendix D). | 26–28 Nov 2026 | Appendix D: record table of the reviewed documents. |

### 5.4.2 Survey

**Design.** The questionnaire has 12 questions in three parts: general information (Q1–Q2), the current process (Q3–Q8) and expectations from a new system (Q9–Q12). Ten of the twelve questions are closed (single choice, multiple choice or 1–5 scales), so that the answers can be counted and shown as charts; Q12 is open text, and Q8 is optional because not every student has borrowed equipment. Q9 asks for the importance of eight candidate features on a 1–5 scale; it is used to check the priorities (MoSCoW) of the functional requirements. Q10 asks whether a short booking ban after repeated no-shows is fair; it is used to check whether the penalty rule R9 is acceptable to users.

**Language.** The survey and the interviews were conducted in Turkish, the native language of most respondents; English translations of all questions are given in Appendices A and B.

**Sampling.** Convenience sampling: the link was shared by the team members in their own class and department messaging groups. Team members were asked not to answer, so that the project team's own views do not affect the results. The sample is therefore not random and may over-represent students of our department; this is stated as a limitation (5.4.5).

**Consent and anonymity.** The form opens with a short information text (course project, anonymous, voluntary, about 3–4 minutes) and a required consent question; answering "No" ends the form. The form does not collect e-mail addresses, names or student numbers, and responses are not limited to one per person because that setting would require signing in. Raw responses are kept in the team's private folder; the report and the public repository contain only aggregated results.

**Analysis.** Only responses with consent "Yes" are analysed. Closed questions are reported as counts and percentages, with the mean for 1–5 scales. Answers to Q12 are grouped into themes by hand and counted. Appendix A lists which chart is drawn for which question and where each result is used (5.2, fishbone, FR priorities, 5.10 operational feasibility, 5.11 benefit inputs).

### 5.4.3 Interviews

**Why these two people.** CampusSlot has two kinds of users: those who book (students and academic staff) and those who manage resources (Resource Admins). The survey reaches the first group only. The interviews cover the second group:

- **INT-01, laboratory staff.** Laboratories and loanable equipment are the main target of CampusSlot, and their bookings are handled by hand today. This interview gives the current process, the approval step for equipment and the staff time per request.
- **INT-02, library staff.** The library already uses an online system for its study rooms. This is an existing solution on our own campus, so the interview shows what such a system solves, what it does not solve (for example, a penalty for repeated no-shows) and what changed for the staff after it was introduced. It also supports the scope decision in 5.2.3 (library study rooms are out of scope) and the comparison of existing solutions in 5.3.

**Form.** Semi-structured, face to face, about 20–30 minutes. Each guide has 10 open questions (L1–L10 for INT-01, K1–K10 for INT-02) and the same five numerical questions (N1–N5: number of resources, requests per week, minutes per request, no-show rate, opening hours). The numerical questions were added because sections 5.8 and 5.11 depend on these figures; an answer the interviewee does not know is recorded as "not known" and never estimated by the team.

**Consent and evidence.** At the start the interviewer explains the purpose and asks for consent to notes and, separately, to an audio recording. The report names only the interviewee's job title. The record form is filled in on the same day. The summary is then confirmed by the interviewee (by e-mail, or read back at the end of the interview if no e-mail address is given). Raw notes and any recording stay in the team's private folder.

### 5.4.4 Observation and document review

**Observation.** One hour of non-participant observation at a busy time of day (planned 12:00–13:00) in a corridor from which 4–6 classrooms or laboratories can be seen. Before the session, the observer finds out which rooms are reserved or scheduled for that hour (door schedules or lab staff). Every 15 minutes (0, 15, 30, 45, 60) each room is recorded as empty or occupied with the number of people. Events are logged with the time: students checking doors or searching for a room, students asking staff, late arrivals, reserved rooms still empty 15 minutes after the start, and rooms used without a visible booking. The 15-minute snapshot matches the no-show limit in R8. The observer does not enter rooms, does not record names and does not photograph people.

**Document review.** The documents DOC-01 to DOC-09 in Appendix D are collected from the interviewees, the room doors, the university and library web pages and the students' messaging groups. Each document is reviewed with the same nine questions: which data it collects, who fills it in and approves it, time and per-person limits, cancellation, no-show rules, conflicts, recorded usage, retention of data, and visible problems. Personal data in any document is covered before the document is stored.

### 5.4.5 Limitations

- The survey uses a convenience sample shared through the team's own groups; it is not representative of the whole university.
- Two interviews give the view of two staff roles, not of every department.
- One hour of observation in one building shows an example, not a campus-wide rate.
- The collection window is short (26–28 Nov 2026) because of the course calendar.

These limits are taken into account when the findings are used: figures from the fact-finding are reported with their source and sample size, and the value analysis (5.11) keeps its sensitivity analysis for the figures that remain uncertain.
