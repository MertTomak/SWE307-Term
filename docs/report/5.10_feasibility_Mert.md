### 5.10.2 Legal feasibility

**Personal data processed.** Name, e-mail, student number, reservation records, check-in records (including time), and penalty/no-show records. Login data (password hash or tokens) is also stored by the authentication feature. These are personal data of students and staff. No special-category data is planned. [TBD: confirm with the architect that none is added]

**Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK).** The articles below were read in the official text on mevzuat.gov.tr (https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatTur=1&MevzuatNo=6698&MevzuatTertip=5, accessed 23 Nov 2026).

| Topic | Article | What the text says | What it means for CampusSlot |
|---|---|---|---|
| General principles | Art. 4(2) | Data must be processed lawfully and fairly, be accurate and up to date, be processed for specific, clear and legitimate purposes, be relevant, limited and proportionate to the purpose, and be kept for the period required by law or by the purpose. | Collect only the fields in the list above. Use them only for reservation, check-in and penalties. |
| Conditions for processing | Art. 5 | Data cannot be processed without explicit consent (Art. 5(1)), except under the conditions listed in Art. 5(2). | The legal basis must be chosen; candidates are listed below. |
| Deletion | Art. 7 | When the reason for processing ends, the data controller deletes, destroys or anonymises the data on its own or on request. Procedure is set by regulation (Art. 7(3)). | A deletion or anonymisation routine is needed (see retention below). |
| Information duty | Art. 10 | At the time data is collected, the controller must tell the person: the controller's identity, the purpose, to whom and why data may be transferred, the method and legal reason of collection, and the rights in Art. 11. | A privacy notice must be shown at registration. |
| Rights of the person | Art. 11 | The person can ask whether data is processed, ask for information, learn the purpose, learn the third parties it is shared with, ask for correction, ask for deletion, and claim damages, among other rights. | The system needs a way to handle access, correction and deletion requests. |
| Data security | Art. 12 | The controller must take the necessary technical and administrative measures to prevent unlawful processing and unlawful access and to keep data safe. | Password hashing, role-based access, HTTPS. Details belong to 5.8 (security). |

**Legal basis for processing (Art. 5).** Art. 5(2) lists these conditions that remove the need for explicit consent: (a) the law expressly provides for it; (b) it is necessary to protect the life or physical integrity of a person who cannot give consent; (c) it is necessary to conclude or perform a contract, and the data belongs to a party of the contract; (ç) it is necessary for the controller to meet a legal obligation; (d) the person made the data public; (e) it is necessary to establish, exercise or protect a right; (f) it is necessary for the controller's legitimate interests, provided the person's fundamental rights are not harmed. The conditions that could fit CampusSlot are candidates only:

| Candidate basis | Possible use in CampusSlot | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 5(2)(f), legitimate interest | Managing shared rooms and equipment, recording check-ins, applying no-show penalties | Needs a balance test against the user's rights. |
| Art. 5(2)(c), contract | Account and reservation data, if accepting the booking terms creates a contract with the university | Depends on how the university treats the terms of use. |
| Art. 5(1), explicit consent | Fallback, for example for optional data | Consent must be freely given, which is hard to show if the service is required for coursework. |
| Art. 5(2)(a) or 5(2)(ç) | Only if a law or a legal obligation requires the processing | Not identified in this draft. |

The choice is **to be confirmed with the university**.

**Retention period.** The regulation on deletion, destruction and anonymisation of personal data (Official Gazette 28 Oct 2017, in force 1 Jan 2018) was read at https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2017/10/20171028-10.htm (accessed 23 Nov 2026). Its provisions used here:
- **Art. 11(1)–(2):** a controller that has a retention and destruction policy deletes, destroys or anonymises data at the first periodic destruction after the date the duty arose. The interval between periodic destructions is set in the policy and "in any case cannot exceed six months".
- **Art. 11(3):** a controller without such a policy must act within three months after the date the duty arose.
- **Art. 12(1)(a):** when a person asks for deletion and all processing conditions have ended, the controller deletes, destroys or anonymises the data and answers the request within 30 days at the latest.

Whether the university is obliged to have a retention and destruction policy was not checked. [TBD: ask the university]

**Proposed retention rule for CampusSlot (a proposal, not a legal finding; to be confirmed with the university):**

| Data | Proposed period | Reason (Art. 4(2), purpose limitation) |
|---|---|---|
| Account data (name, e-mail, student number) | While the account is active. Deleted or anonymised at the first periodic destruction after the account is deleted or the student graduates or leaves, and no later than 6 months after that event. | Needed only to identify the user while the user can book. |
| Reservation and check-in records | Kept for the current academic year; then anonymised (user link removed) at the next periodic destruction. | Needed for no-show decisions and usage reports. |
| Penalty records | Until the penalty ends, then anonymised at the next periodic destruction. | Needed only to apply the penalty. |
| Periodic destruction interval | Every 6 months (the maximum allowed by Art. 11(2)). | Stays within the limit. |
| User deletion requests | Answered within 30 days (Art. 12(1)(a)). | Required by the regulation. |

The periods "current academic year" and the linking of the rule to graduation are our proposal. No source sets them.

### 5.10.3 Schedule feasibility

All figures come from the PERT and critical path analysis in `5.5_planning_Mert.md`.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Critical path | A → B → C → G → L → M → P → Q2 → W → Y |
| Critical path duration | 18.00 days |
| Available time (18 Nov – 6 Dec 2026) | 19.00 days |
| Buffer | 1.00 day (finish at the end of day 18, 5 Dec) |
| Code freeze | EF(P) = 13.50 days against 14.00 days (1 Dec), 0.50 day margin |
| Report PDF | EF(X) = 16.75 days against 17.00 days (4 Dec), 0.25 day margin |
| Probability of finishing on time | about 88.5% (Z = 1.20) |
| Probability of meeting the code freeze | about 75% |

The plan notes that near-critical paths (K, J, Q1 with 0.33 day of slack) are ignored by the normal approximation, so the real probability is somewhat lower. The O, M, P estimates are team judgement.

**Conclusion: Feasible with conditions.** The plan fits in the time, but the buffer is one day and a one-in-four chance of missing the code freeze remains. The condition is that the scope stays fixed and the critical activities (G, L, M, P) are watched daily.
