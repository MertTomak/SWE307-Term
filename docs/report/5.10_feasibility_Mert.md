### 5.10.2 Legal feasibility

**Personal data processed.** According to `backend/prisma/schema.prisma`, the `User` table holds only four personal fields: `fullName`, `email`, `passwordHash` and `role`. A student number is not stored. Keeping only these fields follows the data minimisation principle in KVKK Art. 4(2) (data must be relevant, limited and proportionate to the purpose). Other personal data are reservation records, check-in records (including time), penalty records (including the number of no-shows that triggered them) and in-app notifications. These are personal data of students and staff. The schema contains no special-category data.

**Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK).** The articles below were read in the official text on mevzuat.gov.tr (https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatTur=1&MevzuatNo=6698&MevzuatTertip=5, accessed 23 Nov 2026).

| Topic | Article | What the text says | What it means for CampusSlot |
|---|---|---|---|
| General principles | Art. 4(2) | Data must be processed lawfully and fairly, be accurate and up to date, be processed for specific, clear and legitimate purposes, be relevant, limited and proportionate to the purpose, and be kept for the period required by law or by the purpose. | Collect only the fields in the list above. Use them only for reservation, check-in and penalties. |
| Conditions for processing | Art. 5 | Data cannot be processed without explicit consent (Art. 5(1)), except under the conditions listed in Art. 5(2). | The legal basis must be chosen; candidates are listed below. |
| Deletion | Art. 7 | When the reason for processing ends, the data controller deletes, destroys or anonymises the data on its own or on request. Procedure is set by regulation (Art. 7(3)). | A deletion or anonymisation routine is needed (see retention below). |
| Transfer abroad | Art. 9 (amended by Law No. 7499, Art. 34) | A transfer abroad needs a condition in Art. 5 or Art. 6 **and** one of three mechanisms, in order: an adequacy decision of the Board, or an appropriate safeguard, or an occasional-transfer case. See "Transfer of personal data abroad" below. | The hosting providers in 5.11 (Render, Neon) may store data outside Turkey. This is the open legal point of the project. |
| Information duty | Art. 10 | At the time data is collected, the controller must tell the person: the controller's identity, the purpose, to whom and why data may be transferred, the method and legal reason of collection, and the rights in Art. 11. | A privacy notice must be shown at registration. |
| Rights of the person | Art. 11 | The person can ask whether data is processed, ask for information, learn the purpose, learn the third parties it is shared with, ask for correction, ask for deletion, and claim damages, among other rights. | The system needs a way to handle access, correction and deletion requests. |
| Data security | Art. 12 | The controller must take the necessary technical and administrative measures to prevent unlawful processing and unlawful access and to keep data safe. | Password hashing, role-based access, HTTPS. Details belong to 5.8 (security). |

**Legal basis for processing (Art. 5).** Art. 5(2) lists these conditions that remove the need for explicit consent: (a) the law expressly provides for it; (b) it is necessary to protect the life or physical integrity of a person who cannot give consent; (c) it is necessary to conclude or perform a contract, and the data belongs to a party of the contract; (ç) it is necessary for the controller to meet a legal obligation; (d) the person made the data public; (e) it is necessary to establish, exercise or protect a right; (f) it is necessary for the controller's legitimate interests, provided the person's fundamental rights are not harmed. The conditions that could fit CampusSlot are candidates only:

| Candidate basis | Possible use in CampusSlot | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 5(2)(f), legitimate interest | Managing shared rooms and equipment, recording check-ins, applying no-show penalties | Needs a balance test against the user's rights. |
| Art. 5(2)(c), contract | Account and reservation data, if accepting the booking terms creates a contract with the university | Depends on how the university treats the terms of use. |
| Art. 5(1), explicit consent | Fallback, for example for optional data | Consent must be freely given, which is hard to show if the service is required for coursework. |
| Art. 5(2)(a) or 5(2)(ç) | Only if a law or a legal obligation requires the processing | Not identified in this draft. |

The choice is **to be confirmed with the university**.

**Transfer of personal data abroad (Art. 9).** This point was raised by the System Architect (hand-off H28 / SEC-O1 in `HANDOFFS.md`) and is the most serious open legal issue in the project.

*Why it applies.* The deployment in 5.7 and the cost scenarios in 5.11.2 put the API on **Render** and the database on **Neon**. Neither provider's server region has been chosen or checked. If the data sits outside Turkey, storing students' and staff's personal data there is a transfer abroad and Art. 9 applies in addition to Art. 5.

*Source note.* Art. 9 was rewritten by **Law No. 7499, Art. 34** (accepted 2 Mar 2024; published in the Official Gazette of 12 Mar 2024, No. 32487). Its text was read in full in the official consolidated text of Law 6698 (https://mevzuat.gov.tr/MevzuatMetin/1.5.6698.pdf, accessed 25 Nov 2026), extracted with `pdftotext -layout` (poppler) after an earlier naive extraction had dropped Turkish characters. The article has 11 paragraphs; the two that matter for CampusSlot are quoted below.

> **MADDE 9- (1)** Kişisel veriler, 5 inci ve 6 ncı maddelerde belirtilen şartlardan birinin varlığı ve aktarımın yapılacağı ülke, ülke içerisindeki sektörler veya uluslararası kuruluşlar hakkında yeterlilik kararı bulunması halinde, veri sorumluları ve veri işleyenler tarafından yurt dışına aktarılabilir.
>
> *(Personal data may be transferred abroad by data controllers and data processors if one of the conditions in Art. 5 or Art. 6 is present **and** the Board has issued an adequacy decision for the destination country, a sector within it, or the international organisation.)*
>
> **(5)** Standart sözleşme, imzalanmasından itibaren beş iş günü içinde veri sorumlusu veya veri işleyen tarafından Kuruma bildirilir.
>
> *(The standard contract must be notified to the Authority by the data controller or data processor within five business days of being signed.)*

Paragraph (4) lists the four appropriate-safeguard instruments (non-treaty agreement between public bodies, binding corporate rules, the Board's standard contract, a written undertaking) and paragraph (6) lists the six occasional-transfer cases; both match the implementing regulation's structure described below, so the table in that regulation (read separately, see next paragraph) can be used as the practical guide without re-quoting all 11 paragraphs here.

*The implementing regulation.* "Kişisel Verilerin Yurt Dışına Aktarılmasına İlişkin Usul ve Esaslar Hakkında Yönetmelik", published in the Official Gazette of **10 July 2024, No. 32598** (https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2024/07/20240710-2.htm, accessed 25 Nov 2026). It sets out the three mechanisms, which apply **in this order**:

| Order | Mechanism | Regulation articles | What it requires |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Adequacy decision** (yeterlilik kararı) | Art. 8–9 | The Board decides that a country, a sector in it, or an international organisation gives adequate protection. Reviewed at most every four years and can be changed, suspended or revoked. |
| 2 | **Appropriate safeguards** (uygun güvenceler), if there is no adequacy decision | Art. 10–15 | One of four instruments: a non-treaty agreement between public bodies with Board approval (Art. 11); binding corporate rules for a group of undertakings with Board approval (Art. 12–13); the Board's **standard contract**, used without modification (Art. 14); or a written undertaking with the Board's permission (Art. 15). |
| 3 | **Occasional transfer** (arızi aktarım), only if neither of the above exists | Art. 16 | One of six cases: the person's explicit consent after being informed of the risks; necessity for performing a contract; establishing a contract for the person's benefit; an overriding public interest; establishing, exercising or protecting a right; or protecting life or physical integrity where consent cannot be obtained. |

The **standard contract** is the realistic route for a commercial hosting provider. Regulation Art. 14(5) requires that it be notified to the Authority **within five business days** of the signatures being completed, physically, by registered electronic mail (KEP) or through another method the Board designates; if the parties have not agreed who notifies, the transferring party must do it. Art. 14(6) requires proof of the signatories' authority and certified translations of foreign-language documents.

*Has the Board issued an adequacy decision?* Per Art. 9(2) above, an adequacy decision must be published in the Official Gazette if one exists. Two independent searches of the Authority's own "Yurt Dışına Aktarım" page (kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/2053) converged on the same finding: as of the search date, the page states that no such determination has been made for any country, sector or international organisation. **This is reported with one caveat: `kvkk.gov.tr` serves a broken TLS certificate chain, so the page could not be fetched and screenshotted directly as a clean primary source** (tried 25 Nov 2026, both direct and through a text-extraction proxy). The finding is therefore "very likely true, not independently confirmed with a verbatim quote." [TBD: Mert, open kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/2053/Yurtdisina-Aktarim in an ordinary browser — a normal browser may tolerate the certificate differently than the tooling used here — and either quote the sentence or replace this paragraph if the page has changed] If no adequacy decision exists, mechanism 1 is unavailable and any real deployment outside Turkey would need mechanism 2 (an appropriate safeguard, most realistically the Board's standard contract, Art. 9(4)(c) and 9(5)).

*What this means for CampusSlot.*

| | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Pilot and demo** | **No real personal data leaves Turkey, because only the fictional seed users in `backend/prisma/seed.ts` are stored.** This is the design decision recorded by the System Architect (5.8.5.5, SEC-A3) and it removes the Art. 9 problem for the deliverable the course is graded on. |
| **Before real students use the system** | The university must choose one of: (a) **host in Turkey** — this avoids Art. 9 altogether and is the recommendation of this draft, because it needs no Board instrument and no notification duty; (b) keep Render/Neon and put the Board's **standard contract** in place with each provider, then notify the Authority within five business days; or (c) obtain a different appropriate safeguard. |
| **Not available** | Option (c) in the table above, the **occasional-transfer** route, is not a basis for ordinary hosting. The regulation frames those six cases as incidental; continuous storage of a student body's reservation data on a foreign server is routine processing, not an occasional transfer. |

**Retention period.** The regulation on deletion, destruction and anonymisation of personal data (Official Gazette 28 Oct 2017, No. 30224, in force 1 Jan 2018) was read in its current text on mevzuat.gov.tr (https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatTur=7&MevzuatNo=24038&MevzuatTertip=5, accessed 23 Nov 2026). Its provisions used here:
- **Art. 11(1)–(2):** a controller that has a retention and destruction policy deletes, destroys or anonymises data at the first periodic destruction after the date the duty arose. The interval between periodic destructions is set in the policy and "in any case cannot exceed six months".
- **Art. 11(3):** a controller without such a policy must act within three months after the date the duty arose.
- **Art. 12(1)(a):** when a person asks for deletion and all processing conditions have ended, the controller deletes, destroys or anonymises the data and answers the request within 30 days at the latest.

Whether the university is obliged to have a retention and destruction policy was not checked. Recommendation: confirm with the university IT and legal offices before deployment.

**Proposed retention rule for CampusSlot (a proposal, not a legal finding; to be confirmed with the university):**

| Data | Proposed period | Reason (Art. 4(2), purpose limitation) |
|---|---|---|
| Account data (name, e-mail, role, password hash) | While the account is active. Deleted or anonymised at the first periodic destruction after the account is deleted or the student graduates or leaves, and no later than 6 months after that event. | Needed only to identify the user while the user can book. |
| Reservation and check-in records | Kept for the current academic year; then anonymised (user link removed) at the next periodic destruction. | Needed for no-show decisions and usage reports. |
| Penalty records | Until the penalty ends, then anonymised at the next periodic destruction. | Needed only to apply the penalty. |
| Periodic destruction interval | Every 6 months (the maximum allowed by Art. 11(2)). | Stays within the limit. |
| User deletion requests | Answered within 30 days (Art. 12(1)(a)). | Required by the regulation. |

The periods "current academic year" and the linking of the rule to graduation are our proposal. No source sets them.

### 5.10.5 Economic feasibility

All figures come from the cost–benefit analysis in `5.11_operating-costs_Mert.md`. The base case is the paid hosting scenario (Scenario B), a 3-year horizon and a 36% nominal discount rate, counting only the administrative time saved as a benefit.

|---|---|---|

| Net annual benefit | 54,383.40 TL/year | 5.11.7 |
| Discount rate (TCMB 1-week repo, 23 Oct 2026) | 36% | 5.11.4 |
| **NPV (3 years)** | **+19,697.01 TL** | 5.11.8 |
| ROI, discounted (3 years) | 11.27% | 5.11.9 |
| ROI, undiscounted (3 years) | 35.78% | 5.11.9 |
| Simple payback | 1.31 years | 5.11.10 |
| Discounted payback | 2.09 years | 5.11.10 |

**Conclusion: Feasible with conditions — but the conclusion is not yet evidence-based.** The NPV is positive in the base case and positive in every scenario and horizon combination tested in 5.11.11(b). However:

- The benefit side rests on parameters proposed by the PM and **not measured** (assumptions C13 to C16 in 5.11.1). The cost side is sourced; the benefit side is not.
- The result is decided by one parameter: the staff time one manual booking takes today (C15). **The NPV changes sign between 8 and 10 minutes per booking** (5.11.11a). At 5 minutes the project does not pay for itself over three years at any discount rate in the tested range.
- The discount rate is almost irrelevant by comparison: ±5 percentage points moves the NPV by about 6,000 TL, while ±2 minutes per booking moves it by about 40,000 TL.

**The condition is therefore a measurement, not a technical or legal step:** Zeynep's survey and admin interview (due 25 Nov 2026) must establish the booking volume, the current handling time per booking and the current no-show rate. Until then the economic case is a model with a known break-even point, not a finding. The base case was deliberately built as the least favourable defensible combination (paid hosting, shorter horizon, only the hard benefit), so new evidence is more likely to improve the result than to worsen it.

Note also that the benefit is freed staff time, not cash revenue (5.11.12). It becomes a real saving only if the university reassigns that time or avoids a hire.
